openOMSI

Privacy

Applies to openOMSI on every platform, including the iPhone, iPad and Mac apps. Draft update 1 November 2026 (pending operator verification).

The core game does not require an openOMSI account and does not include advertising or analytics SDKs. Your settings, saved games, screenshots and the OMSI 2 content you add are normally kept on your device. Optional online services and multiplayer do process some technical and player information, as explained below.

"Playing now" counter

While a drive runs, the game tells the project's counter service every few minutes that one more copy of openOMSI is being played, and says goodbye when the drive ends. It sends a random id made for that session alone (a new one every start, not stored on your device), the game's version and the kind of system (for example iOS). It sends no name, no device identifier and nothing about what you play. The service forgets an id 25 minutes after its last message and does not store IP addresses. The website shows the resulting count. You can switch this off in the launcher: Settings, General, "Count me on the website's 'playing now'".

Online features you choose to use

  • Multiplayer: when you host or join a session, the game sends your chosen driver name, vehicle and position, chat messages you send, and technical connection information. The host receives the network information needed to connect you. A host may also receive a persistent hardware-derived identifier used by the server's ban system. Most sessions are hosted and administered by players rather than by the project. Finding or joining a session may involve public listing or relay services (including ntfy.sh and Cloudflare tunnels); those services can also process connection metadata according to their own policies.
  • Real weather and internet radio: if you turn them on, the game fetches weather reports (aviationweather.gov) and radio streams directly from those services. They see an ordinary request from your network address.
  • Downloads: mods, translation models or other files you choose to download come from the sites they are hosted on (for example GitHub or Hugging Face).

Multiplayer safety and logs

Individual game and dedicated server operators can retain their own logs. In current releases, ordinary LAN logs may include player names and chat message text. Do not publish raw server logs or diagnostic files without removing private information. Multiplayer administrators can kick and ban participants and enforce voice-chat restrictions. The game offers profanity filtering and chat rate limits, but these do not detect every kind of harmful content. Players can turn off voice chat, which is disabled by default.

Ban-related device identifiers may be persistent even when hashed. They should be handled as potentially personal information, with restricted access and retention appropriate to the hosting operator's responsibilities.

Community applications and creator forms

A Discord community and any optional creator application form are separate from playing the core game. Before submitting a form, check the notice provided by its operator for the purposes, lawful basis, recipients, retention period and a private contact channel for data requests. The game privacy notice alone does not describe the actual handling of form responses. Please do not post personal data or form submissions in public support channels.

Children

The core game does not currently verify a player's age. Online play can involve communication with other players, and hosting operators may make their own moderation and safety decisions. Age checks and specific protections depend on the service and legal requirements; we do not ask every player to provide identity documents merely to run the offline game. This notice is not a guarantee that every independently hosted session is suitable for children.

Contact

For general questions, see the project's GitHub issues or the community Discord. Those channels may be public: do not send private account details, device identifiers, identity documents, private logs, or sensitive data there. The project operators must publish an appropriate private contact channel for data-subject requests once responsibility and routing are confirmed.
